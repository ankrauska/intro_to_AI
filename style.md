# Writing style

How the manuscript may be written. Read before drafting any prose. When in doubt, prefer the simpler, more direct sentence.

Add your own rules at the bottom. Old reviewer criticism belongs there too: if a reviewer once complained about it, write the rule down.

## The main rule

**No claim without a result behind it.** Every claim in the results and discussion traces to a number in `analysis/output/` or to a checked reference in `references.bib`. A sentence that sounds right but traces to nothing gets deleted, not softened.

## Reporting results

- Report an effect size and a confidence interval, not an adjective. "4.2 points higher (95% CI 1.1 to 7.3)", not "substantially higher".
- Say what the interval includes. If it includes effects too small to matter clinically, say so.
- Give the denominator. "31% of participants (42 of 135)", not "a substantial proportion".
- Do not call a non-significant result "no difference". Write "no difference was detected" and give the interval.
- Do not call an association a cause unless the design supports it.

## Limitations

- State limitations plainly, with their likely direction. "Those lost to follow-up were more often from lower-income households. The estimate is therefore likely biased upward."
- Do not "mitigate" a limitation in the next sentence. Do not call your own methods "robust".

## Banned words and phrases

groundbreaking, paradigm shift, profound, novel (unless it is), crucial, critical importance, pivotal, underscores, highlights the importance of, sheds light on, delve, leverage, robust, holistic, landscape, multifaceted, it is worth noting that, notably, importantly, in conclusion (in the discussion), a testament to.

## Voice

- **First person plural, declarative.** "We included 135 children." Not "135 children were included in the study".
- **Concrete subjects.** Lead with the thing doing the work. Not "There is..." or "It should be noted that...".
- **No bare "This" as a subject.** "This suggests..." becomes "The difference suggests...".
- **No throat-clearing.** Cut "In this section, we will...". Just say it.

## Sentence shape

- **Short sentences carry the argument.** Several short sentences, not one long sentence with three commas.
- **One idea per sentence.** A population, an outcome and a method are three ideas.
- **Fuller paragraphs.** One idea per paragraph, developed. Not one sentence per paragraph.

## Punctuation

- **No em-dashes as clause separators.** Use a period or a colon.
- **Parentheses sparingly**, for short glosses and cross-references. Do not stack two in one sentence.

## Structure

- Do not make every paragraph three sentences, or every list three items. Symmetry is a sign of generated text.
- Prefer an argument in prose to a bullet list in the introduction and discussion.
- Do not hedge on top of hedges. One honest statement of uncertainty is enough.

## Before and after

> **Before:** Our findings underscore the critical importance of early intervention and highlight a paradigm shift in how we conceptualize auditory rehabilitation, with profound implications for clinical practice.
>
> **After:** Children implanted before 12 months scored 4.2 points higher on the language measure at 36 months (95% CI 1.1 to 7.3). The interval includes effects too small to be clinically meaningful.

> **Before:** While some limitations exist, it is worth noting that the robust methodology employed largely mitigates these concerns, and the results remain highly compelling.
>
> **After:** Follow-up was incomplete for 31% of participants, and those lost were more often from lower-income households. The estimate is therefore likely biased upward.

## Language

Instructions to the model can be in Danish. The manuscript is in English unless `plan.md` says otherwise. Check that clinical terms are the ones your field uses, not literal translations.
